Public sources, citations, licenses, and access obligations

This page identifies the external knowledge sources and benchmark families named by ESLM's frozen source manifests. It gives a reviewer the publication to cite, selected data release, governing license or access rule, and immutable repository identity. Benchmark scores remain in timestamped result artifacts so source identity and attribution stay stable across runs.

Execution status is generated: use the evaluation dashboard or the raw v2 report for catalog-declared adapter and evaluation state, access state, executed scope, scores, and receipt currentness. A catalog state describes the registered route; only an execution receipt plus its checkpoint audit establishes what actually ran and whether that result still describes the current worktree. A source listed here may be acquired and validated without having a current execution result.

Four records are required for one reproducible source

The publication explains the research object and supplies the scholarly citation. The dataset release supplies the bytes. License and access terms determine permitted use and redistribution. A checksum identifies the exact cached artifact consumed by a validated adapter or knowledge-package compiler. No edge transfers rights from one node to another: in particular, an open-access paper does not make its associated dataset freely redistributable.

A source citation is not a substitute for a license, and a license label is not a substitute for complying with access conditions. Cached files stay under ignored training/.cache/ paths. Test labels remain evaluation oracles and are not promoted into knowledge packages or exposed to synthesis agents. If a delivered archive carries terms that differ from this page, the delivered terms control and the source manifest must be updated before use.

Additional public benchmark families

The inventory below describes frozen source identity and acquisition obligations, not adapter or execution state. Inclusion means that a source manifest identifies the artifact; it does not assert that a benchmark route exists or ran. Where an archive contains an answer or preference label, any compliant adapter must separate the visible task from the host-only oracle before the symbolic runtime receives the item.

BLiMP

Research citation. Alex Warstadt, Alicia Parrish, Haokun Liu, Anhad Mohananey, Wei Peng, Sheng-Fu Wang, and Samuel R. Bowman. “BLiMP: The Benchmark of Linguistic Minimal Pairs for English.” Transactions of the Association for Computational Linguistics 8, 2020, pp. 377–392. DOI: 10.1162/tacl_a_00321.

Data and license. ESLM uses the official BLiMP repository at commit 3e56b06fcabca9b30822fc66435fca6b1aa40bb1. The pinned source archive has SHA-256 cbada5cc59b41798f0f0a6b2525166c7a1d82c4a40ed726c78810b898e1979f6. The repository README declares CC BY 4.0. A BLiMP item is a labeled grammatical minimal pair; the pair label is an evaluation oracle and is never language evidence for a candidate KB.

bAbI

Research citation. Jason Weston, Antoine Bordes, Sumit Chopra, Alexander M. Rush, Bart van Merriënboer, Armand Joulin, and Tomas Mikolov. “Towards AI-Complete Question Answering: A Set of Prerequisite Toy Tasks.” arXiv:1502.05698, 2015.

Data and license. The adapter uses the English 10k files from the tasks 1–20 v1.2 archive, whose SHA-256 is 84f5296ab9a1ad0dc9464e08c491d65cd08830fca3acae9ab86f75e0fb81573c. The archive’s own LICENSE.txt declares CC BY 3.0 Unported. Story statements and questions are visible task input; expected answers and supporting-line identifiers are held by the evaluator. Training-visible source policy that is genuinely reusable may be represented in babi-v1.2-language, but individual benchmark answers may not.

Story Cloze and ROCStories source family

“Story Cloze” does not identify one interchangeable file. A reviewer must record which release supplied the stories and which construction supplied the candidate endings. The public Rochester landing page requires a submitted access form and does not state dataset-specific license or redistribution terms. The operator completed that workflow and supplied the delivered Winter 2018 links. ESLM's explicit acquisition command follows only the allowlisted delivered redirect chain, validates the exact artifacts, and stores them in ignored local cache; it does not redistribute delivered files. The CC BY 4.0 license shown by the ACL Anthology applies to the scholarly papers hosted there; it does not grant rights over ROCStories or Story Cloze dataset files.

ROCStories and the original Story Cloze evaluation

Exact citation. Nasrin Mostafazadeh, Nathanael Chambers, Xiaodong He, Devi Parikh, Dhruv Batra, Lucy Vanderwende, Pushmeet Kohli, and James Allen. “A Corpus and Cloze Evaluation for Deeper Understanding of Commonsense Stories.” Proceedings of the 2016 Conference of the North American Chapter of the Association for Computational Linguistics: Human Language Technologies, San Diego, California, June 2016, pp. 839–849. DOI: 10.18653/v1/N16-1098.

This paper introduces ROCStories, whose records are five-sentence commonsense stories, and the Story Cloze evaluation, which supplies a four-sentence context and asks a system to choose between two candidate fifth sentences. Use this citation for ROCStories and for the original Spring 2016 or subsequent ROCStories/Story Cloze releases derived from that research program. In addition to the paper, identify the delivered set name and release in any experimental report; “Story Cloze” alone is not an adequate artifact identifier.

The 2018 bias-reduced Story Cloze construction

Exact citation. Rishi Sharma, James Allen, Omid Bakhshandeh, and Nasrin Mostafazadeh. “Tackling the Story Ending Biases in The Story Cloze Test.” Proceedings of the 56th Annual Meeting of the Association for Computational Linguistics, Volume 2: Short Papers, Melbourne, Australia, July 2018, pp. 752–757. DOI: 10.18653/v1/P18-2119.

This paper documents the ending-only biases identified in the earlier construction and a new crowdsourcing procedure intended to reduce them. Cite Sharma et al. whenever an experiment uses the 2018 bias-reduced Story Cloze set or makes a claim about that construction. Also cite Mostafazadeh et al. when the experiment relies on ROCStories contexts or compares with the original Story Cloze task. The two references answer different provenance questions and neither removes the requirement to retain the access terms delivered with the data.

Delivered release inventory. The operator received the following official short links after completing the access workflow. The frozen ESLM registration identifies only the recommended Winter 2018 validation and test files as selected artifacts; the other links are provenance records, not claims that those data were acquired or used.

  • Story Cloze Winter 2018: validation set and test set.
  • Story Cloze Spring 2016: validation set and test set.
  • ROCStories full five-sentence corpora: Winter 2017 and Spring 2016.

Access procedure for any Story Cloze set

Begin at the official ROCStories and Story Cloze landing page and submit its access form. Store the delivered archive, the delivery message, and the terms in an ignored local cache. The frozen registration selects the delivered Winter 2018 validation and test sets, not the separate Spring 2016 or ROCStories corpus files. A compliant adapter records exact filenames, release name, byte counts, checksums, schemas, split identities, and the absent redistribution grant. Do not infer that one form submission covers every later set, and do not expose correct-ending labels to training or candidate-generation subprocesses.

SimpleQA

Research citation. Jason Wei, Karina Nguyen, Hyung Won Chung, Joy Jiao, Spencer Papay, Mia Glaese, John Schulman, and Liam Fedus. “Measuring short-form factuality in large language models.” arXiv:2411.04368, 2024. The accompanying OpenAI description is “Introducing SimpleQA.”

Data, code, and evaluation boundary. ESLM pins the official simple-evals repository at commit 652c89d0ca9df547706735883097e9537d40dc47 and the public simple_qa_test_set.csv, 2,012,910 bytes, SHA-256 feee3f7e7db3617e94e8fcf1977b756ec420ef8568f4e0fcbbe0e92e9d5fc032. The repository is MIT licensed. Questions may be visible to evaluation; reference answers remain local-evaluator-only and are forbidden from synthesis packets. The official evaluation uses semantic CORRECT, INCORRECT, and NOT_ATTEMPTED judgments. Local exact matching is only a diagnostic and must not be described as an official SimpleQA score.

Reviewer checklist

QuestionRequired evidence
Are these the intended bytes?Match release or commit, artifact name, byte count where declared, and SHA-256 against the source manifest before parsing.
May the data be downloaded automatically?Acquisition is always an explicit host operation, never a side effect of asking a question. Every selected source needs a pinned local source record, and redistribution rights remain source-specific. ZebraLogic's private oracle and EWoK require accepted terms and authenticated access. ReClor is limited by its official noncommercial condition; LogiQA, ProverQA, ProofWriter, and several other releases have no explicit dataset license identifier and remain local without row redistribution pending clarification. CLUTRR uses its non-commercial research release. Entity Tracking has upstream redistribution restrictions. Story Cloze uses the official form-delivered files.
May rows be committed or redistributed?Apply the dataset’s own license and terms, not the paper’s license. Keep gated, password-protected, form-delivered, non-commercial, and no-explicit-license data out of the repository unless its terms explicitly permit publication.
Can labels guide implementation?Only development-visible failures may guide generalizable changes. Hidden or test labels remain evaluator-only and cannot appear in agent packets, knowledge records, parser rules, or handcrafted item exceptions.
What must an experimental report cite?Cite the primary publication, identify the exact dataset release and split, preserve the license-required attribution, and report the immutable artifact identity. For Story Cloze, identify whether the original construction or the 2018 bias-reduced construction was used.
