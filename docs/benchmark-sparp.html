SpaRC and SpaRP: controlled property sets for spatial reasoning

SpaRP reorganizes spatial examples into property sets that isolate object type, relation completeness, quantification, and relation families. ESLM reports each property set separately because point vectors, extended-object inequalities, and topological relations are different semantic problems.

Complete source and overlap

The pinned official release contains 24 unique content-addressed artifacts exposed through 36 logical paths. ESLM stores each byte-identical official alias once, streams every top-level JSON array, and retains 416,678 unique rows. The complete test population remains untouched by reasoning execution. Dataset rights are CC BY-SA 4.0; official code is Apache-2.0.

PS2, PS3, and PS4 are property-controlled transformations and mostly nested subsets of StepGame. A label-free overlap audit hashes only visible context and question fields and shows that every unique StepGame-derived PS2–PS4 item occurs in the corresponding StepGame source. These rows are useful controlled evidence but not an independent sample that may be added to StepGame's denominator.

Property-set semantics

PS2 describes quantified, relation-complete point objects and uses exact vector constraints. PS3 removes explicit quantification while remaining relation-complete and point-based; its declared policy invalidates opposed steps rather than treating all local directions as an exact global coordinate equation. PS4 describes extended objects and uses start/end boundary inequalities plus an explicit overlap policy on unmentioned dimensions. Both core methods return replayable witnesses and are documented in the spatial reasoning chapter.

PS1 combines extended objects with incomplete relations and includes horizontal, vertical, and front/behind directions; near and far; disconnection, external connection, and partial overlap; and tangential and non-tangential proper-part relations in both directions. Point vectors and boundary ordering alone cannot express this relation family. The adapter therefore preserves all fifteen source relations in a declarative-qualitative-relation-system-v1 and emits a finite qualitative-relation-task-v1. The generic executor sees opaque relation identifiers, reciprocal inverse declarations, binary composition rules, exclusivity groups, facts, and ordered query endpoints. It does not see the benchmark identity or answer.

Reciprocal inverse declarations reverse directional and proper-part facts. A relation whose declared inverse is itself is symmetric; this is how near, far, disconnection, external connection, and partial overlap receive their reverse edges without making symmetry a built-in core assumption. Binary rules declare directional transitivity, including front/behind, and proper-part nesting. Nesting any two tangential or non-tangential member links yields a non-tangential member link because contact with an outer boundary is not preserved through an intermediate region; the converse holder relations follow the reciprocal declaration.

Containment lifting is selective. Horizontal, vertical, front/behind, far, and disconnection relations may lift from holders to their members. Near, external connection, and partial overlap do not lift: two regions being near, touching, or partially overlapping does not imply that arbitrary objects contained by those regions bear the same relation. These permissions and exclusions are adapter-owned rules derived from the declared source calculus and development-visible evidence. The generic closure engine has no spatial word constants and cannot invent an undeclared composition.

Qualitative closure and proof replay

The executor validates the finite relation system, inserts each asserted fact and its declared inverse, then processes a queue of newly known subject–relation–object triples. For a queued edge, it joins compatible incoming and outgoing edges at their shared entity. A declared binary rule may add its result relation over the two outer endpoints. Newly added triples return to the queue, so the process computes the least fixed point of the declared inverse and composition system, subject to explicit bounds on facts, entities, relation types, rules, derivations, and proof depth. No matching relation produces UNKNOWN; two entailed relations in one declared exclusivity group produce INCONSISTENT_CONTEXT; malformed data and exhausted limits remain separate outcomes.

Every returned relation carries a tree. An asserted leaf identifies the accepted fact. An inverse node records the reversed endpoints and reciprocal relation. A composition node records the rule identifier, intermediate entity, and its two premise trees. Verification recursively replays those nodes against the accepted facts and declarations. It then recomputes the complete fixed point and compares the ordered output set, which detects both fabricated evidence and a result that omits an entailed relation.

Scoring and interpretation

The current PS1 measurement is development evidence, not a protected-test claim. On the complete full validation configuration, 2,397 of 2,397 tasks match the complete multi-label target and all 2,397 proof sets replay successfully; exact accuracy and macro-F1 are both 1. The run covers 1,204 one-hop, 787 two-hop, 361 three-hop, and 45 four-hop tasks. Its answers contain one relation in 1,606 cases, two in 334, three in 300, and four in 157. The separate 500-row small validation view is also 500/500 with 500 verified proof sets. The official PS1 test split has not been executed.

The candidate was promoted only after the genericity audit passed. A subsequent complete validation execution routed all 2,397 PS1 tasks through EslmEngine.executeTask, selected method:core:declarative-qualitative-relation-closure in every case, reproduced the same exact outputs, and replay-verified every proof. The runtime binding therefore uses the same public typed-task boundary as the other spatial methods.

PS3 passes all 4,499 full validation cases and PS4 passes all 3,775, with a verified witness for every result. The exact vector method also solves and verifies all 4,976 PS2 cases, but matches 4,907 official targets. In the remaining 69 cases, the official target disagrees with the replayable vector sum entailed by the same record's relation-complete quantified symbolic context. ESLM retains the derived answer and records a source-oracle incompatibility instead of adding row-specific reversals. The dashboard reports exact match and executed scope, while the source receipt also retains macro-F1 because SpaRP is multi-label.
