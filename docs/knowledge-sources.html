ESLM Documentation / Knowledge sources

Real sources, generated modules, and future queue

Knowledge corpora supply persistent lexical, factual or commonsense evidence. Benchmark datasets supply questions and oracles. ESLM stores them in different directories, records source exposure in results, and never converts a catalog entry into a claim that training occurred.

Why the two built sources use different semantics

WordNet records lexical senses and typed lexical relations. A lemma may map to many synsets, and a hypernym path can license a bounded taxonomic proof. A definition remains cited text. ATOMIC records event-centered continuations with PersonX/PersonY placeholders. An xIntent continuation is a possible actor intention; an xEffect is a possible actor effect; HinderedBy is a possible obstacle. Flattening both sources into untyped triples would lose ambiguity, direction and epistemic status.

Source exposure and evaluation

The 700-case random suite samples generated WordNet and ATOMIC structures and verifies their execution. It is a source-exposed self-consistency/regression test, not an independent comparison with an LLM. A future WordNet- or ATOMIC-derived benchmark must label source exposure. ATOMIC dev/test are deliberately uncompiled and may support held-out tests once the question adapter and protocol are frozen.

Why ConceptNet waits

The next source is much larger and combines multiple contributors, languages, weights, relations and licenses. Current public models are split into generated files but imported eagerly. Their roughly 0.6 GB combined RSS growth shows that adding ConceptNet with the same approach would be careless. Before ingestion, runtime planning must select a profile and shard from the question without assembling unrelated source records.
