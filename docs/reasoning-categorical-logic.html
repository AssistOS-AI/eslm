Categorical propositions, immediate inference, and syllogistic models

The categorical method is an executable fragment of traditional term logic. It represents universal and particular affirmative and negative propositions, applies declared one-premise transformations, and decides two-premise syllogisms by exhaustive model semantics. Its outputs follow typed premises and finite models independently of mood names, benchmark rows, and familiar nouns.

Every finite population that satisfies the typed premises is retained. A conclusion is returned only if it holds in every retained population; the witness reports the model count and selected categorical form.

Four proposition forms

Let S and P denote unary classes over a domain D. ESLM uses the traditional existential-import interpretation shown below. This differs from the modern first-order convention in which a universal can be vacuously true when its subject class is empty. The distinction is explicit because conversion per accidens and subalternation depend on it.

FormSurface template and implemented condition
AAll S are P: S is non-empty and every member of S is a member of P.
ENo S are P: S is non-empty and no member of S is a member of P.
ISome S are P: at least one domain member belongs to both S and P.
OSome S are not P: at least one domain member belongs to S and not to P.

A term carries a base identifier and a complement depth. Membership in non-P is Boolean negation of membership in P; an even number of complements cancels and an odd number remains. The original depth is retained in the typed input and transformation witness, while realization emits the parity-normalized term. This is an algebraic normalization, not a lexical rule about an English prefix.

The traditional square of opposition

A and O are contradictories, as are E and I. A and E are contraries. I and O are subcontraries. A subalternates to I and E subalternates to O. Consequently a true A makes I true, but a true I leaves A undetermined. The executor applies the complete table only after proving that premise and candidate share the same typed subject and predicate literals.

The historical and modern interpretations of the square differ precisely over existential import. Useful context includes the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry on the traditional square of opposition, the overview of the square of opposition, and the broader history of term logic. ESLM's operative semantics is the table and model condition above, not an unstated appeal to tradition.

Immediate transformations

OperationExecutable rule and invalid boundary
ConversionExchange subject and predicate. E and I convert simply; A converts per accidens to I; O has no valid conversion.
ObversionReverse affirmative/negative quality and complement the predicate: A↔E and I↔O. Every form has a valid obverse.
ContrapositionExchange the terms and complement both. A and O are valid; E and I are rejected.

The implementation returns the input and output proposition structures as a transformation witness. A reversible-equivalence closure uses obversion for all forms, simple conversion for E and I, and contraposition for A and O. It deliberately excludes A-to-I conversion and subalternation because they preserve entailment in one direction, not logical equivalence.

Two-premise syllogism by exhaustive populations

A valid input contains exactly three atomic terms and one term shared by the two premises. With three terms there are eight possible Boolean membership valuations for one individual. A population is a non-empty subset of those valuations, so the executor checks 255 possible populations. It retains a population only when both categorical premises and the declared existential commitments hold.

The endpoint terms are the two terms that occur once; the shared term is eliminated as the middle term. The executor constructs both endpoint orientations, positive and complemented endpoint literals, and all four conclusion forms. Candidates are ordered toward lower complement depth and stronger categorical information. The first candidate that holds in every retained population is returned. The completeness claim applies exactly to this declared three-term model space; broader monadic first-order proof requires its own method and acceptance evidence.

Meaning-preserving transformations need careful existential accounting. Under the traditional interpretation, contraposing All S are P preserves the class-inclusion condition but the original existential commitment remains S. The adapter therefore records the original existential literal as semantic transformation provenance. The core consumes that declared commitment and never infers it from a source category or expected conclusion.

Logical validity and selected-gold distinction

The prompt “derive the categorical relationship logically entailed by these premises” does not always determine one form. If A is entailed, I is also entailed under existential import; if E is entailed, O is also entailed. The development source contains alpha-isomorphic structures for which one row selects A and another selects I, or one selects E and another selects O. Vocabulary, row order, and source identity are the only remaining distinctions, and none is logically admissible.

ESLM therefore returns the strongest low-complement conclusion and reports strict source-gold equivalence separately from logical soundness. Supplying a requested mood would make the desired form observable, while accepting every sound entailed conclusion would make the scorer match the natural-language prompt. The formal proof and resolution choices are recorded on Exceptions and issues.
