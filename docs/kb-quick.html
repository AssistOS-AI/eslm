QUICK: the small development knowledge base

quick exists so a developer can start ESLM immediately and inspect every premise behind an answer. It combines the child-basic, animals, and space-geography fixtures into one generated graph. QUICK is authored project data, not public-corpus training, and its results are never presented as broad knowledge or public benchmark evidence.

What it is for

QUICK tests the parts of the runtime that large corpora also depend on: module loading, entity merging, fact deduplication, rule closure, source provenance, exceptions, session overlays, natural-language normalization and deterministic realization. Because the graph is small, a failing proof can be understood without searching millions of records. It is loaded by default in interactive mode and may be removed with /unload quick.

Commands and tested examples

node src/cli.mjs ask "Jhon is a man. Is Jhon going to die?" --kb quick
node src/cli.mjs ask "Can Penguin swim?" --kb quick
node src/cli.mjs ask "Where is Neptune?" --kb quick
node src/cli.mjs kb show quick
node src/cli.mjs kb validate quick

The source manifest is training/KBs/QUICK/source/manifest.json. Its three reviewed source files live beside it, and the generated bundle is rooted at training/KBs/QUICK/model/manifest.mjs. Rebuilding QUICK also rebuilds the three components, merges them by semantic identity, regenerates all postings and validates the final graph.

What QUICK does not establish

Its facts were not extracted from WordNet, ATOMIC, ConceptNet, Wikipedia or another external authority. Its tiny selection does not establish coverage, factual currency or general commonsense. It is useful precisely because its evidence boundary is narrow and visible. Use WordNet for source-derived lexical knowledge and ATOMIC for source-derived event hypotheses.
